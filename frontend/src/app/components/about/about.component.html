About

Most of my time goes to secure multiparty computation — protocols that let several parties compute a shared result over data none of them is willing to hand over.

What draws me to it is the gap between what we can prove and what actually gets deployed. Moving a protocol from paper into practice tends to break it in one of two ways: either a foundational assumption that does not hold outside the model, or security traded away for the efficiency a system needs to be usable at all. Narrowing that gap is what I am after.

Both failures show up in my work. Security proofs assume a fixed share of participants will simply follow the rules; real ones are self-interested and collude when it pays. So I design mechanisms that make honesty the rational choice rather than an assumption. Real networks, meanwhile, offer none of the timing guarantees protocols are written against — messages arrive late, out of order, or never. There I work on dispersing and storing information reliably anyway, and on what that resilience costs elsewhere in a protocol.

Outside of research, I wakeboard whenever there is water and a free afternoon, and I follow both kinds of football — the Chicago Bears and Arsenal.
